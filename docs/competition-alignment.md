# Competition context and requirement mapping

[Project overview](../README.md) · [Design case study](case-study.md) · [My contribution](my-contribution.md)

## Competition and chosen track

The project was developed for the **2025 Sustainability Expertise Case Competition**, sponsored by the **Center for Professional Responsibility in Business and Society (CPRBS)**. The team selected **Pillar 3: The User Experience (Dashboard & Visualization Design)** and proposed a redesign of the existing Gies Sustainability Dashboard.

The brief allows teams to focus on at least one pillar and states that the elements listed under Pillar 3 should each be addressed explicitly. The track covers a first-glance landing page, external pages that could direct traffic to it, advanced visualizations, and journeys for two specified personas.

The source is the organizer's competition brief, supplied by the project lead as `CPRBS Case Comp Flyer 2025 - final.docx`. This page paraphrases its relevant design requirements. The original brief's description of the existing platform's data collection and LLM classification provides context; those systems are not claimed as work implemented by this team. All data in the team's prototype is simulated.

## Requirement mapping

| Requirement | Project evidence and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Identify three to five pieces of immediate landing-page information | Home has four primary KPI cards: publication volume, top-journal percentage, SDG share, and faculty engagement. Additional SDG highlights are a secondary information layer. |
| Provide a wireframe or mockup of the landing page | The interactive Figma Make homepage and its [screenshot](../images/dashboard-home.png) show the proposed layout. |
| Consider external pages that link to the landing page and how to attract traffic | An external referral-page or traffic-acquisition proposal was **not included in the original submission**. It is not presented as a completed deliverable. |
| Propose visualizations beyond standard bar and pie charts and explain their purpose | The existing dashboard includes quadrant views, a faculty network, color-coded state tiles, and a state-by-SDG heatmap. The [visualization gallery](case-study.md#visualization-gallery) documents those original views and explains the comparisons they support. |
| Map a prospective student's search for a sustainable-finance faculty mentor | The article search, filters, faculty discovery, and profile exploration form the intended workflow. The portfolio case study applies the original general student journey to this specified task. |
| Map a donor's interest in renewable-energy research | SDG 7 / clean-energy exploration, donor views, hypothetical funding inputs, illustrative stories, and comparisons form the intended workflow. The case study applies the original general donor journey to this task. |
| Identify navigation, search, filters, and drill-downs for the journeys | The prototype provides a Guide and module navigation, article keywords, SDG / department / year controls, faculty profiles, and donor-module views. The case study connects them to the tasks. |

The team also designed a **university leadership** journey. This extends the work to another audience named in the competition's broader context, alongside the two specified persona tasks.

## What external referral-page design means

This requirement concerns how visitors discover and enter the dashboard from another page. A proposal could describe the source page, the intended audience, the placement and wording of an entry point, and where that visitor should arrive inside the dashboard. It can involve UI mockups and a rationale for the referral path.

The project did not include that proposal. The Guide and internal navigation demonstrate movement between dashboard sections; they do not establish external referral pages or measured traffic acquisition.

## Prototype scope

The brief considers innovation and technical feasibility, with slightly greater emphasis on innovation. It identifies working prototypes as a possible source of bonus credit. The portfolio demonstrates an interactive UX proposal with simulated data; it does not establish production integration or measured research, fundraising, or usability results.
